# Statistical and scientific significance review

**Scope and independence (15 October 2026).** Independent statistical review of the five most decision-relevant primary papers among the project's 13 audits, plus the executed Dallas two-subject pilot. I examined the versioned primary texts and the project's paper audits and execution receipt; I did not inspect Claude's review, rerun authors' training code, or reanalyze restricted benchmark cases. Results below are *reported* outcomes unless explicitly identified as a mathematical deduction or a local pilot result. This is a cross-paper evidence audit under the project's [Norman-style protocol](../methodology/NORMAN_PAPER_READING_PROTOCOL.md), not the original external skill.

## Main objections to causal interpretation

1. **Vero's mixed-task effect is real within its reported setup but not a uniquely identified mechanism.** Its Table 2 reports +5.8 overall for equal ratios against +5.2, +5.2 and +4.8 for three alternative allocations. This compares specific mixtures on one initial model; it does not isolate category *balance* from the available examples, verifier coverage, reward variance or open-ended exposure. The Appendix A.5 mixture analysis uses five domains at 20% each; the headline Vero-600K uses six domains. Do not treat the five-domain allocation ablation as an MRI-optimal ratio. Figure 7's single-task versus mixed transfer result supports within-paper negative transfer, but an MRI causal claim needs same starting weights, subject-disjoint examples and equal updates, rollouts, unique-example exposure and visual-token budget. Vero's own §8 describes its trace-behavior analysis as descriptive, not causal. Table 4c's 54.7 versus 54.3 GSPO/GRPO average is too narrow to establish GSPO necessity for MRI.

2. **MedGRPO's proposed raw-scale explanation is underidentified by group-relative normalization.** For rewards within one prompt/group, define \(A_i=(r_i-\bar r)/(s_r+\epsilon)\). Under \(r'_i=a r_i+b\), \(a>0\), and negligible epsilon, \(A'_i=A_i\): shifting or multiplying every reward in that group by a positive constant cannot by itself rebalance tasks after exact per-group standardization. With nonzero epsilon, clipping, prompt-wise weights, reward mixing, or implementation-specific advantage handling, this invariance can fail slightly or materially. A *nonlinear* logistic transform can change within-group margins for groups of three or more, while for a two-sample non-tied group standardized signs are unchanged. Zero-variance groups are decisive: all-equal rewards yield zero advantage with a standard epsilon implementation, or may be skipped; if the transform leaves equality intact, it cannot create gradient information there. Task-specific zero-variance frequency and its handling must be logged. MedGRPO Table 3's dramatic full-versus-unnormalized collapse is a reported implementation result, but it does not establish that cross-task raw scale caused it. Compare raw, positive affine, logistic, rank/quantile, clipping and zero-variance filtering with identical sampled groups before a new optimizer claim. Table 2's Next Action Prediction decrease (0.442 SFT to 0.405 MedGRPO) is a warning against reporting only a macro gain.

3. **LoV3D cannot attribute high diagnostic accuracy to MRI interval reasoning.** Table 1 reports 93.7% diagnosis and 82.6% region accuracy on its ADNI test set; Table 2's SFT-to-DPO changes are much smaller (93.3→93.7 and 81.6→82.6). The system has MMSE, CDR-SB, APOE and, at follow-up, prior FreeSurfer-derived regional labels; its cited 58.9% image-only CNN comparison lacks these inputs. Thus the large comparison is not an image-only architecture effect. The 88.2% longitudinal direction on 316 follow-up scans is a within-study metric against processed targets, not independent proof of observed tissue change. Subject-disjoint ADNI split is stated; seed variation, leakage across external cohorts, protocol-stratified uncertainty, metadata-only and prior-label-only comparisons are **NOT_VERIFIED**. A new MRI project must beat these shortcut controls, not merely match a diagnosis number.

4. **NeuroQA sets a demanding shortcut baseline and illustrates reference heterogeneity.** Its Table 2 test-public closed text-only floor is 49.4%, while the best zero-shot VLM closed result reported there is 47.5%. Table 4's 44.6% text-only number is for the full pool, so these denominators cannot be mixed. The 1,166 longitudinal questions are genuine paired-scan items, but some benchmark categories are image-*informed* from FreeSurfer or clinical instruments rather than visually answerable. The 100-item clinician comparison gives only seven longitudinal items in Appendix F, and Appendix G has one jointly rated longitudinal open answer; neither validates all longitudinal targets clinically. Its Wilson interval at the *question* level does not by itself handle clustering of multiple questions per subject. Split claims are subject-based within the benchmark; cross-source identity, source-image license, and external pretraining overlap remain **NOT_VERIFIED**.

5. **BrainTRACE measures evidence fidelity under a specific rubric, not a training effect.** Tables 2–3 report best broad closed 41.9%, open factual pass 19.8%, and full-case success 1.4%. Table 4's full/current-only/no-image comparison is valuable, but the 120-item set was selected to have a 50% full-evidence baseline; its differences are a controlled diagnostic for that selected set, not a population effect estimate. All questions from one patient must be grouped for uncertainty. The report-derived reference may omit scan findings, while segmentation-derived measurements inherit processing error. Appendix C.5's 60-step neuroradiologist audit and C.6's judge-family sensitivity mean open-ended ranks depend on evaluator choice. A native-volume versus rendered-slice leaderboard comparison changes input interface as well as representation. Dataset account and judge version differ between v1 paper and evolving repository: **SOURCE_MISMATCH** until pinned.

## Correct unit, estimand and uncertainty for our experiment

The unit of generalization is the **patient**, with all visits, modalities, questions, derived measurements and rendered views clustered inside that patient. A scan, question, slice or rollout is not an independent patient. Define task-specific patient scores first, then estimate paired differences between two frozen models on the same held-out patients. Report patient-cluster confidence intervals (and preferably seed-to-seed variation for training), cohort/site strata, per-task denominators, abstentions and adverse regressions. With only two Dallas subjects, no meaningful patient-population interval, longitudinal sensitivity estimate, subgroup comparison or model-training split is possible. The local eight-volume result establishes file integrity, NIfTI decoding and display geometry. One FLAIR pair changes through-plane spacing from 6 to 2.5 mm, so an apparent difference could be acquisition rather than biology. FreeSurfer cross-sectional volume change is a processed estimate, not a diagnosis or independently validated progression label.

For longitudinal direction, predeclare a target measurable on both visits; use a separately QC'd longitudinal pipeline or adjudicated reference, and classify changes within a prespecified test–retest/reproducibility interval as *indeterminate*. Report conditional accuracy on resolvable changes **and coverage** among all eligible patients, since excluding hard cases can inflate accuracy. Stratify scanner/protocol changes and acquisition interval. For spatial claims, use native voxel/world geometry, independent annotations and 3D metrics when claiming volumetric grounding; frame IoU measures 2D localization only.

## Image-use and evaluator controls

The minimum *frozen-model* ladder precedes RL: question/metadata only; current scan only; both true visits; both visits reversed; wrong-prior scan from a different patient; modality removal; and an image-measurement pipeline with the same allowed inputs. Freeze answer format, prompt, renderer, slice selection, visual-token budget and decoding where possible. A correct temporal mechanism should improve on both-visits input, reverse direction when order is reversed where appropriate, and degrade with a wrong prior. If text-only matches paired-image performance, the item is not evidence of visual reasoning. If a model can read a FreeSurfer target from supplied metadata, the reward is shortcut-maximizable.

Keep three evaluators separate: (i) deterministic training verifier, (ii) independent quantitative or expert reference, and (iii) benchmark scorer. Do not call agreement with the training verifier clinical correctness. For open reports, blind expert adjudication or a separately calibrated factual rubric should check omissions, laterality, interval direction and unsupported claims; report judge-family sensitivity. **BrainTRACE is sealed evaluation-only:** no use of its test questions, hidden references, scores or error analyses to tune rewards, prompts, render settings, checkpoints or stopping. Before a single final evaluation, freeze the model and analysis plan, verify DUA access and cross-source patient lineage, and pin benchmark dataset, scorer and judge versions. If these conditions cannot be met, mark BrainTRACE `DATA_BLOCKED`, not a training or validation source.

## What would matter, and decision rules

| Level | Evidence that would matter | Insufficient evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Scientific MRI significance** | Better patient-level identification of independently adjudicated spatial findings or repeatability-qualified interval change, with a clinically meaningful margin, acceptable false assertions and coverage, including scanner/protocol strata. | A higher question average, prettier reports, a FreeSurfer-derived reward score, or two illustrative scans. |
| **AI/ML significance** | A reproducible negative-transfer mechanism: balanced training protects off-target patient outcomes beyond same-data SFT, single-task RL, proportional mix, simple reward/advantage normalization and open-ended exposure, across seeds and held-out sites. | Porting Vero routing/GSPO to MRI or beating an unnormalized, badly calibrated GRPO baseline alone. |
| **Experimental significance** | Patient and source separation, equal compute/data/visual inputs, independent endpoint, image-removal and visit-swap controls, transparent missingness and all prespecified categories. | Model-size or metadata mismatch, question-level pseudo-replication, evaluator trained on the same signal, or selectively reported tasks. |

**GO (feasibility):** expand to an adequately sized, rights-cleared, patient-linked real MRI cohort and audit task labels and verifier failure modes without training. **GO (RL study):** only after an image-use effect and independent reference survive the frozen-model controls, run equal-budget SFT, single-task RL and balanced/proportional RL with the MedGRPO-inspired calibration controls above. Predeclare a minimum clinically or scientifically meaningful effect and an allowable regression margin *before* seeing test outcomes. **REFORMULATE:** if paired-image gains vanish after text-only/current-only/wrong-prior controls, if change labels fall inside repeatability bounds, or if logistic/variance handling alone explains the apparent multi-task benefit. **KILL the specific methodological claim:** if the patient-cluster interval excludes the preregistered meaningful gain, if there is unresolved patient leakage, if the verifier rewards impossible/unsupported answers, or if strong same-data SFT/simple normalization matches balanced RL. These decisions are conditional research gates, not claims that any model has passed them.

## Evidence status

The five papers' numeric findings are **published reports, not local replications**. Our Dallas status is `TESTED_CPU` for ingestion/rendering and `NOT_RUN` for model inference or RL training. No source here establishes a new neuroscience finding or clinical deployment readiness. A full 13-paper statistical meta-analysis is **NOT_VERIFIED** because commensurate effects, independent units and variance estimates are unavailable.
