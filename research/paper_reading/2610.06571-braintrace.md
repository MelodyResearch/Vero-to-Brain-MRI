## Layer 1 — Research process

The authors diagnose a gap between isolated image accuracy and evidence-preserving follow-up interpretation. They retain MR-RATE patients with at least three MRI studies plus reports and segmentation-derived measurements (Sec. 3.3; Appendix B.2, PDF pp. 4, 16). Human-in-loop task construction separates model-facing images/questions/allowed metadata from hidden report and measurement references (Fig. 3, PDF p. 5). Five reasoning levels span acquisition recognition through interval comparison, trajectory and case synthesis. Three tracks contain 5,653 broad-compatible items, 1,200 3D-evidence items, and 70 cases × six steps = 420 case items (Sec. 3.2, PDF p. 4). The benchmark is not an optimizer, reward-training method, or released longitudinal MRI training set.

Closed questions use normalized exact match. Open/case items use a factual audit: required facts, critical rubric slots, no critical contradictions and no unsupported material claims must all pass (Appendix C.1, PDF pp. 18–19). Case success multiplies the six step-pass indicators within each patient. Non-native models receive rendered multi-slice images; native-volume baselines use their own interfaces, so comparisons do not isolate representation (Sec. 4.1; Appendix D.1, PDF pp. 7, 27).

## Layer 2 — Evidence audit

- **Direct Evidence, within the benchmark:** Table 2 (PDF p. 7) reports best broad closed-form score 41.9%; Table 3 (PDF p. 8) reports best broad open-ended pass 19.8% and best full-case success 1.4%. These are benchmark/rubric measurements, not absolute clinical performance.
- **Conditional Evidence:** Table 4 (PDF p. 9) drops full-evidence accuracy from a constructed 50.0% on 120 matched items to 12.5/26.7% for latest-timepoint-only (Qwen3.5-27B/GPT-5.4), and to 15.0/9.2% for no-image. The sample was selected to have a 50% baseline; it is a mechanistic diagnostic, not a population effect estimate (Appendix E, PDF pp. 28–29).
- **Conditional Evidence:** Two neuroradiologists graded 60 case steps; judge–human weighted κ = .737 and .645, versus inter-human .821; binary critical-error κ = .475/.552, versus inter-human .767 (Appendix C.5, PDF p. 23). The GPT judge's pass rate on 52 GPT-5.4 outputs was 32.7% versus 15.4% for two other judge families (Appendix C.6, PDF pp. 23–24). Exact open-ended ranks are judge-sensitive.
- **Not Established:** The reference is report-grounded, not an independent de novo scan read; reports may omit findings and segmentation values carry processing error (Appendix G, PDF p. 35). The native-volume versus rendered-slice comparison is interface-confounded. Broadly low scores do not show that a particular RL algorithm will fix them.

## Layer 3 — Next experiment

Keep BrainTRACE **sealed evaluation-only**. On a separate patient-disjoint public MRI training/validation cohort, build a paired-input model and pre-register a simple no-image/current-only/both-visits test. Freeze checkpoint, prompt, render settings and model selection before one BrainTRACE evaluation. Report per-level, per-evidence-tag, patient-clustered uncertainty, laterality and interval-direction errors; disclose interface differences. If paired visual input does not improve the longitudinal subset over current-only/no-image, the proposed temporal mechanism is falsified. If factual pass remains at benchmark floor despite other gains, reformulate the reward/representation; never use BrainTRACE hidden targets to tune reward, prompts or checkpoints.

**Decision:** GO as a held-out audit target only, subject to DUA and provenance reconciliation; not a training source.

## Independent novelty and MRI transfer

BrainTRACE already defines real repeat, multi-sequence, report-grounded MRI evaluation and evidence-channel ablations. Our project cannot claim novelty for introducing such an evaluation axis. A contribution must be a new *method* that improves patient-independent evidence preservation and case synthesis beyond a frozen baseline, with matched compute and no benchmark tuning. A 2D mosaic or higher aggregate score alone is insufficient.
