# LoV3D — longitudinal 3D brain MRI with normative verification

## Verified metadata and access

Zhaoyang Jiang et al., *LoV3D: Grounding Cognitive Prognosis Reasoning in Longitudinal 3D Brain MRI via Regional Volume Assessments*, [arXiv:2603.12071v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.12071), dated 14 March 2026. Full 13-page PDF checked. The paper links [code](https://github.com/Anonymous-TEVC/LoV-3D); its README says weights await acceptance, so runnable weights are **NOT_VERIFIED**. Publication venue is **NOT_VERIFIED**; repository calls it a MICCAI 2026 submission. Scope is T1 structural MRI in cognitive diagnosis, not multi-sequence lesion follow-up.

## Layer 1 — Research process

**Problem and unit.** The authors seek an anatomically interpretable CN/MCI/dementia output from repeated T1 scans. They use ADNI; after FreeSurfer completeness filtering, train/validation/test contain 3,993/525/479 scans from 2,059/258/258 subjects. The test set has 316 follow-up scans. This is a genuine repeated-scan design with a claimed subject-level held-out split, though complete cross-cohort identity linkage is not independently audited here (Sec. 3.1).

**Inputs and causal chain.** T1 images are skull stripped, MNI registered, normalized, resampled to 128³. A 3D ResNet-50 maps a scan to 512 visual tokens; an MLP projects into Qwen-2.5-14B. The prompt also includes demographics, APOE, MMSE, CDR-SB and, on follow-up, prior FreeSurfer anatomical labels. Current-scan FreeSurfer values are withheld from the prompt but used as verifier targets (Fig. 1; Sec. 2.1). Stage 0 trains encoder volume regression; Stage 1a aligns the projector; Stage 1b uses LoRA SFT; Stage 2 scores four sampled outputs and optimizes best versus worst with DPO, β=0.1 (Secs. 2.3–2.6).

**Verifier.** Training-split CN subjects fit region-specific age/sex normative regressions on ICV-normalized volumes, yielding z-scores (Eq. 1). Thresholds at −0.5 and −1.5 assign normal/mild/severe labels, with ±0.25 z tolerance. The weighted verifier checks anatomical categories, diagnosis, interval direction, text/label consistency, and summary alignment (Eqs. 2–4). These are FreeSurfer-derived surrogate truths and logical constraints, not independent clinical adjudication. The paper's irreversibility rule (C2) flags a two-level apparent improvement; this requires caution under scanner or segmentation drift (Sec. 2.2).

## Layer 2 — Evidence audit

**Direct Evidence, reported performance.** Table 1 (PDF p. 8) gives 93.7% three-class diagnostic accuracy and 82.6% region accuracy on the 479 ADNI test scans; the same pipeline without Stage 0 region grounding gives 92.5% and 80.7%. Table 2 (PDF p. 9) gives Stage 1b→DPO diagnostic accuracy 93.3→93.7 and region accuracy 81.6→82.6; BLEU-4 rises .354→.584 and false-severe rate falls 4.1→2.2. Longitudinal direction accuracy is reported as 88.2% over 316 follow-up scans (Sec. 3.3, PDF p. 9). **Conditional Evidence:** Table 3 (PDF p. 11) reports MIRIAD 95.4% per scan on binary AD/HC and AIBL 82.9% three-class accuracy, but baselines use different training cohorts and/or class mappings. No uncertainty intervals or multi-seed variance are shown in the cited tables.

**Not Established / independent interpretation.** The leap from a 58.9% image-only CNN to 93.7% LoV3D does **not** isolate MRI reasoning: LoV3D sees MMSE/CDR-SB/APOE while the CNN comparison is image-only (Sec. 3.2, PDF pp. 7–8). The clean ablations are much smaller (+1.2 pp for region grounding; +0.4 pp diagnosis for DPO). The result supports a multimodal structured-output system on this split, not proof that the image or interval comparison caused the high diagnostic score. BLEU/ROUGE summarize wording overlap, not clinical factuality. Cross-sectional FreeSurfer targets and age-norm thresholds can be noisy; external scanner invariance is **Author Interpretation**, not isolated by a scanner-only intervention. The paper itself limits scope to T1, FreeSurfer targets and broad MCI categories (Conclusion, PDF p. 11).

**Novelty for our project.** Structured anatomical verification, prior-region labels, longitudinal coherence and preference optimization are already prior art here. Our MRI work cannot claim to invent them. A distinct contribution would require measured advantage from *image-grounded interval evidence* under modality/metadata controls, with robust subject-separated uncertainty.

## Layer 3 — Next experiment

On a patient-disjoint repeated-T1 set, fix preprocessing, model, prompts, budget and seed; evaluate the same checkpoint with (a) current image + clinical metadata, (b) both images + the same metadata, and (c) both images with prior anatomical labels removed. Pre-register a within-patient change target from independently QC'd longitudinal processing, not the same FreeSurfer output used to form the reward. Compare interval-direction accuracy and error type at the **subject** level. If (b) does not improve over (a), the proposed visual longitudinal gain is falsified. If removing clinical scores causes most diagnostic accuracy to vanish, diagnosis was metadata dominated. Include scanner/protocol strata and negative controls with swapped or time-reversed pairs; a model that retains performance on them has not learned temporal anatomy.

**Decision:** REFORMULATE any claim that high diagnosis accuracy proves image-based interval reasoning. Useful verifier design and staged training precedent; not a direct multi-sequence or Vero-style GRPO recipe.

## Independent novelty and MRI transfer

Keep LoV3D as the closest novelty boundary for repeated T1. A novel MRI method must outperform its metadata/prior-label/current-image controls on independently measured interval change, not simply reproduce its structured output or DPO.

No local code/weight run, site-matched image-only ablation, cross-cohort identity audit, multi-seed replication, or independent clinical re-read was verified.
