# NeuroQA — primary-source audit

## Verified metadata and access

`arXiv:2605.20525v1`, submitted 19 May 2026: *NeuroQA: A Large-Scale Image-Grounded Benchmark for 3D Brain MRI Understanding*, Mohammad H. Abbasi, Favour Nerrise et al. ([abstract/version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20525), [full text and appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20525v1), [v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.20525v1), [official code](https://github.com/mhabbasiit/neuroqa), [Tier 1 dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mhabbasiit/NeuroQA)). This is a **benchmark and deterministic data-construction release**, not an RL method. I read the full arXiv HTML including appendix. The web reader rejected the PDF on size, but an independent [source reconciliation](SOURCE_RECONCILIATION.md) downloaded the exact v1 PDF and verified the key page pointers below. The GitHub README differs from v1 on first-round review item count and reports a different shortcut margin; this is a **SOURCE_MISMATCH between paper and evolving repository**, so the versioned paper is used for reported experiments.

## Layer 1 — Research process

The authors target a weakness of earlier medical VQA: correct closed answers may come from template/dataset priors while 2D slices cannot convey whole-brain anatomy or repeat-visit change (§§1–2). The exact gap is a multi-domain **3D brain MRI evaluation** with subject-separated splits, shortcut controls, and clinician reference. They use 12 cohorts and 203 templates across 11 reasoning categories, generating 56,953 QA pairs for 12,977 subjects (§3.4). Every item links to an MNI-space volume; some include T2 and actual prior scan plus acquisition date (§4.1). Image-grounded items are distinguished from image-*informed* items whose exact answers come from FreeSurfer volumetry or clinical instruments (§3.1). Their chosen deterministic templates and 38-rule audit remove LLM generation variance, while cohort-specific thresholding and answer balancing reduce text shortcuts (§3.3). This replaces free-form synthetic QA with reproducible label derivation, but it does not make every target visually recoverable.

Source data and derived ground truth span FreeSurfer, structured metadata, DICOM headers, and negation-aware radiology-report parsing (§3). Candidate generation (72,603 pairs) is followed by expert review, machine audits, and shortcut refinement; Figure 2, PDF p. 6. Public Tier 1 has 19,262 QA over 2,467 subjects, while restricted Tier 2 has 37,691 over 10,510 (§3.2, PDF p. 5). Subject-level train/validation/test-public/test-private splits are 40,355/5,493/5,524/5,581 items (§3.2, PDF p. 5). Their baseline design includes text-only majority-template, chance, two clinicians on 100 frozen items, a 1.02M-parameter supervised 3D CNN, and several zero-shot VLMs (§4). No Vero-style optimizer, reward, or model architecture is proposed. A useful alternative would be independent clinical adjudication of longitudinal ground truth, at higher expense; another is a narrower fully open cohort to maximize reproducibility but reduce disease coverage.

## Layer 2 — Evidence audit

| Claim | Primary evidence | Judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Text priors are reduced | §3.3 stage 5, PDF p. 6; Table 4, PDF p. 9: full-pool closed text-only 44.6% (95% Wilson CI 44.1–45.1), random 39.5%; test-public text-only 49.4% (Table 2, PDF p. 8). | **Direct Evidence** for the paper's defined majority-template control; more sophisticated text-only models may differ. |
| Current VLMs fail to clear text-only | Table 2, PDF p. 8: best zero-shot Gemini-3.1-Pro 47.5% closed vs 49.4% text-only on test-public; supervised 3D CNN 43.7%. | **Direct Evidence** for these baselines and decoding setup. CNN vs VLM is not size/data matched; performance ranking is not an architecture causal test. |
| Clinicians show a perceptual ceiling | Tables 2–3, PDF p. 8: 100-item two-rater mean 48.9% closed, κ=0.40; image-grounded 50.0% across 62 items, image-informed 42.1% across 38. | **Conditional Evidence**. Two resident raters, small per-category cells, and some labels are not visually recoverable by design. |
| Longitudinal capability is tested | Appendix Table 14, PDF p. 27: 1,166 longitudinal items from 14 templates. §4.1, PDF p. 7, supplies prior scan/date. | **Direct Evidence** for repeated-scan questions in the benchmark; **Not Established** that any evaluated model can answer reliably. |
| Entire benchmark is image-grounded | §3.1: 131 templates image-grounded, 72 image-informed. | **Not Established**; the title's broad reading conflicts with the explicit distinction. |

The paper's 49.4% text-only number is a **test-public floor**, whereas 44.6% is the **full-pool** result; they must not be interchanged. The provided full 3D volume need not mean VLMs encode native 3D: §4.1 permits sampled orthogonal 2D slices. The main closed aggregate combines cohorts, modalities, and categories; per-category analysis is necessary. Subject separation is stated and enforced internally, but cross-dataset person identity, external pretraining exposure, and image distribution shift are **NOT_VERIFIED** independently here. Dataset-derived medians are not clinical cutoffs; FreeSurfer changes are not independent adjudication of disease progression. Appendix G validates answer string scoring with **non-clinician raters** and has only one jointly rated longitudinal item; it cannot certify longitudinal clinical validity. Appendix F's seven longitudinal clinician items are too few for strong stability claims. Appendix A itself flags WMH T1 items that need T2/FLAIR-aware scoring.

The [code README](https://github.com/mhabbasiit/neuroqa) currently says the first **question-only** expert round had 57 items, whereas arXiv v1 Appendix E.1, PDF p. 17, says 24. The separate **image-paired** second round did have 57 items (Appendix E.2, PDF p. 20); that count is not itself contradictory. The README also reports text-only margin 5.6 points vs the paper's 5.1 (Table 4, PDF p. 9). These should be reconciled against a pinned release before claiming exact independent reproduction. The paper's versioned statistics above are not replaced with the README numbers.

## Layer 3 — Next experiment

**Question:** Can a model use paired MRI to infer quantitative change, rather than answer from wording, dates, or demographic priors? **Hypothesis:** a registration-aware two-visit input improves change-direction accuracy over text-only, single-visit, and visit-swapped controls on independent subjects, especially beyond a prespecified test–retest uncertainty margin. First run those controls on the public, properly licensed subset with true repeat visits. Use the published paired images and verified acquisition order, plus a separately recomputed quantitative measure or independent adjudication where available. Compare base model, a measurement pipeline, and the same VLM with both visits; keep architecture/decoding fixed. Primary outcome: **subject-level** paired direction accuracy conditional on change exceeding repeatability bound; report abstentions and cohort-specific results. Ablate date text, registration, modality availability, and visit order. GO only if two-visit input beats all shortcuts on held-out people; REFORMULATE if gain vanishes after thresholding or appears only on image-informed clinical-instrument questions; KILL the proposed visual-change claim if text-only or reversed visits perform equally.

## Independent novelty and MRI transfer

**Novelty location:** benchmark construction, shortcut-aware evaluation, deterministic validation and governance-aware release. **Inherited:** FreeSurfer measurements, MNI registration, template QA, existing VLMs and CNNs. **Necessary difference:** same item connects whole volume, clinically explicit evidence type, subject-disjoint split, and image-absent/fabrication/hallucination stress tests (Figure 4, PDF p. 9). It **already includes longitudinal paired-volume questions**. Thus our project cannot claim first longitudinal brain MRI VQA or novelty from simply adding a second scan. Potential new methodological space is verifier-correct 3D or longitudinal *training* with registration, uncertainty and capability-preservation controls, beyond a NeuroQA benchmark score. Treat NeuroQA test and test-private as evaluation-only; never tune a reward on them. Minimal superiority result: on an independent subject cohort, a balanced RL model yields verified paired-change and spatial gains beyond same-data SFT/single-task RL and text-only/swap controls, without report regression.

## Missing evidence and uncertainties

Key v1 PDF page numbers above were verified by independent source reconciliation; there was no independent rerun of the data pipeline or code. The three-part image-grounding stress test is specified in §4.5 and Figure 4, PDF p. 9, but per-model pass/fail results were not established in the paper excerpt reviewed; leaderboard status is **NOT_VERIFIED**. No evidence here of model training via GRPO/GSPO. Source-image licensing remains governed by original cohorts, not Tier 1 QA license.
