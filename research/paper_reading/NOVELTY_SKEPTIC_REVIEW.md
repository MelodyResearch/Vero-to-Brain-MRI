# Independent novelty skeptic review

**Review date:** 15 October 2026. **Scope:** the 13 individual audits in this directory, plus direct spot checks of versioned primary papers and official repositories for Vero, LoV3D, BrReMark, MedGRPO, NeuroQA, and BrainTRACE. This is an independent prior-art and experimental-design review; I did not inspect another reviewer's synthesis or Claude's output. I did not run author code, download protected MRI, independently reproduce a paper result, or exhaustively search all unpublished work. Numbers below are *author-reported*, not our replications.

## Fast verdict

**REFORMULATE the novelty claim.** “Apply Vero's balanced categories and routed rewards to brain MRI” is an application recipe, not a new optimization method. A credible *candidate* contribution is an acquisition-aware, subject-linked verifier for **actual paired-volume change**, together with a controlled test of whether a balanced RL mixture improves that capability beyond equally supervised SFT, single-task RL, ordinary reward normalization, and strong prior methods. The verifier needs independent change targets and negative controls; an implementation alone does not establish novelty or validity. A useful negative result could still establish that Vero-style balancing does not transfer under MRI constraints.

## Objections and corrections to emphasize across the audits

1. **Do not call balanced categories an MRI-specific method.** Vero's own [Fig. 7 and §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04917v3) already test mixed versus single-category RL with equal 100K sample count. MedGRPO already normalizes different medical task rewards. The untested MRI question is *whether* the effect persists with valid MRI targets and matched reward/group statistics, not whether the general design exists.
2. **Do not equate group-relative advantage with solved scale confounding.** GRPO normalizes sampled responses *within a prompt*, while MedGRPO additionally transforms reward distributions per dataset-task. Task-specific rates of all-correct/all-wrong groups, informative gradients, verifier noise and sample duplication can still differ. Before training, count nonzero-variance groups and reward error on adversarial examples; during training, match active groups and tokens, not just nominal examples.
3. **Do not use LoV3D's headline 93.7% diagnostic accuracy to infer image-based interval reasoning.** Its [§2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12071v2) passes clinical scores and prior FreeSurfer labels to the model; its [Table 1/2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12071v2) do not provide a matched metadata-only versus both-images versus current-image comparison. The within-pipeline diagnosis gain from Stage 1b to DPO is only 0.4 percentage points, although that comparison also changes other output metrics. A clinical-metadata shortcut remains live.
4. **Do not claim a novel 3D verifier from 2D box IoU.** BrReMark selects the lesion-maximal slice with a reference mask; SGMRI-VQA evaluates frame-indexed 2D boxes; MedVol-R1 uses masks plus a propagator. None verifies physically located voxel change across registered visits. Conversely, a volume measurement created by the *same* segmentation pipeline as the reward is a self-consistency check, not independent biological truth.
5. **Do not call the absence of one exact combination a positive novelty result.** The plausible gap is *joint* acquisition-aware, image-dependent paired change learning and capability preservation under a controlled task mixture. That is **NOT_VERIFIED** as novel across all literature and **NOT_YET_DEMONSTRATED** in this project. Search and experiment must remain open to a stronger prior result.
6. **Treat benchmark and implementation drift as real limits.** BrainTRACE's arXiv paper and [scoring repository](https://github.com/lanqz7766/BrainTRACE) identify different dataset accounts and judge defaults in the existing audit. Until pinned, no exact reproduced BrainTRACE score should be claimed. NeuroQA's report/clinical-instrument-derived targets and FreeSurfer thresholds require evidence-type stratification; an aggregate score may reward metadata or threshold retrieval rather than visual change.

## What remains untested, and what could count as a new mechanism

The **untested scientific question** is whether a model reads *registered, repeated, multi-sequence volumes* and makes change claims that depend on the correct anatomy, sequence and time order, under acquisition variation. The **untested ML question** is whether task mixing prevents off-target loss beyond what equal data exposure, SFT, reward calibration and removal of uninformative rollout groups achieve. These are hypotheses, not findings.

A **candidate methodological mechanism** would be a verifier that scores a model's explicit physical-space evidence chain: subject/visit linkage; sequence identity; left/right and voxel-to-world coordinate; region or lesion correspondence after quality-controlled registration; magnitude and direction relative to test-retest/segmentation uncertainty; and a defensible `uncertain/indeterminate` response when change is below that threshold or acquisition differs. The reward must be falsifiable on swapped visits, wrong subject, wrong sequence, left-right flips, misregistration and acquisition/protocol shifts. Existing work covers pieces; the conjunction is worth testing only if it delivers a capability that LoV3D, BrReMark, NeuroQA-style SFT and a simple measurement-rule baseline cannot.

## Minimum falsifier, before expensive RL

1. Build a **small, sealed, subject-disjoint** paired-scan test with QC'd 3D images, visit dates, sequence/acquisition metadata, region/lesion references and an independent change target or uncertainty interval. Dallas's two verified subjects are an access/geometry smoke test, not an inference sample or clinical truth set. Keep NeuroQA/BrainTRACE test material out of reward tuning.
2. Run a **verifier challenge set before model training**: correct pair; reversed order; prior removed; wrong-subject prior; wrong-sequence image; left-right flip; known registration error; protocol-shifted but biologically stable repeat; and below-repeatability change. Blind the verifier to answer-template cues where possible. Primary outcome: per-subject false acceptance of an incorrect change claim and true acceptance of an independently supported change, stratified by acquisition shift. A verifier that awards the same score to swapped/wrong-pair evidence **kills the proposed image-grounded longitudinal reward**.
3. If that gate passes, freeze one model, data and prompt format. First compare base, same-data SFT, metadata/current-only input, paired-image input, and a deterministic measurement-rule baseline. If paired images do not beat current-only/metadata under patient-level inference, **reformulate the visual-change claim** before RL.
4. Only then compare same-data single-task RL, natural-proportion multi-task RL, balanced multi-task RL, and balanced RL with reward normalization/active-group matching. Keep starting checkpoint, image-token budget, unique patients, training labels, rollouts, updates and evaluation fixed; repeat seeds. Measure a prespecified paired-change endpoint, 3D grounding endpoint and open-ended/report regression separately. A balance gain that disappears with normalization or stronger SFT **kills the claimed balanced-RL mechanism** even if the system remains a useful application.

**GO** for careful verifier and baseline experiments. **REFORMULATE** any assertion of a novel Vero optimizer, first longitudinal brain MRI VLM, first medical multi-task reward, first 3D MRI QA, or first MRI spatial RL. **KILL** the proposed *method claim* if the challenge set fails, or if matched SFT/ordinary normalization/strong prior baselines equal the RL gain; this does not preclude a useful dataset or negative methodological result.
