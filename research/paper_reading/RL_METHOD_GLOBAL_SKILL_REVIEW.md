## Layer 1 — reconstruct the methods

| Method | Problem and design logic | Operative method | Credible alternative / failure condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **GSPO** | Token importance ratios can be volatile when a reward is assigned to a whole response, particularly under MoE expert-route changes. The source paper proposes sequence-level clipping to match reward granularity. | In [§4, Eqs. 5–7](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.18071v2), group reward advantage is `(r_i − mean_g r)/std_g r`; sequence ratio is `s_i = exp(mean_t log[pi_theta(y_it)/pi_old(y_it)])`; a single clipped ratio weights each response. [§4.3, Eqs. 13–17](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.18071v2) gives a token-graph implementation numerically and gradient-equivalent when every token receives the same advantage. | Token GRPO with well-controlled likelihood recomputation and clipping tests whether sequence ratio itself matters on dense MRI VLMs. GSPO's original MoE-routing stability rationale may not transfer to a dense model. |
| **Vero v3** | Broad 2D visual reasoning needs heterogeneous answers and preservation of open-ended chat, beyond math-only RL. Its design selects/filter/canonicalizes data, balances categories, routes ten answer types, then uses inherited GSPO-family optimization. | [§3.3 Eq. 2 and Appendix B.2 Eqs. 6–9](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04917v3) use the GSPO-token form with constant per-sequence advantage, tiny asymmetric sequence-ratio clips `0.0003/0.0004`, no KL, and a soft overlong penalty. [Eq. 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04917v3) combines 0.8 accuracy, 0.2 format and overlong terms. The ten routes in §3.3 include exact, numeric, ordering, 2D box, click, instruction and LLM judge. | The strongest MRI alternative is same-data SFT plus independently valid 3D/task-specific verifiers and calibrated sampling, before assuming online RL. Vero's generic 2D reward routes cannot validate voxel masks or repeated-scan biological change. |
| **MedGRPO v4** | Medical video tasks differ in metric and difficulty; the authors aim to make heterogeneous GRPO learning stable after medical-video SFT. | [§3.2 Eqs. 1–2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06581v4) use per-prompt standardized reward with *token-level* GRPO ratios; [§3.3 Eq. 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06581v4) first transforms each dataset-task metric `x` by `sigmoid(3(x−p50)/IQR)`, with SFT training-set percentiles. A medical LLM judge is mixed with caption similarity. | Compare raw reward, positive affine scaling, logistic, rank calibration, and zero-variance filtering at fixed task sample/compute budgets. The main text specifies upper clip 0.3 while §4.1 says 0.28; exact implementation is **UNVERIFIED**. |
| **MT-GRPO v3** | Equal nominal task weights can yield unequal effective gradients when tasks differ in all-correct/all-wrong rollout frequency; pure worst-task emphasis can collapse task weights. | [§3.2, Subroutine 1, Eq. 8 and §§4–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05547) jointly update policy and task sampling weights using task reward plus improvement, then enforce desired *post-filter* category counts through ratio-preserving, acceptance-aware oversampling ([Algorithm 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05547)). Its source code is [adapted veRL with Experiment 1/2 scripts](https://github.com/rsshyam/MT-GRPO). | Fixed uniform + ratio-preserving sampling is a strong simple baseline. Dynamic worst-task weighting must also be evaluated at a fixed wall-clock/rollout budget because extra resampling has cost. Paper experiments are text reasoning, not MRI or Vero data. |

**Mathematical mechanism, independently derived.** For a group of responses to *one prompt*, if `r'_i = a r_i + b` with `a>0` and the denominator is exactly the group standard deviation, then `A'_i=A_i`: positive affine dataset-wise scale and offset disappear. With implementation `std+epsilon`, the equality is approximate, and breaks when epsilon or numerical precision is material. MedGRPO's sigmoid is monotone but nonlinear; for groups of at least three distinct values it can change relative advantage margins and saturation. For a two-response group with distinct rewards and no epsilon, any monotone transform gives the same standardized sign/magnitude. If all rewards are equal, `std=0` and any deterministic transform of each equal value remains equal: no calibration can create a learning signal. Binary exact-match MRI tasks with eight rollouts may therefore produce many all-right/all-wrong groups. Filtering, resampling, curriculum or informative graded rewards must be tested explicitly. The paper's claim that logistic nonzero derivative gives “smooth gradients” [§3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06581v4) describes the mathematical reward transform, but ordinary policy-gradient training treats sampled rewards as constants; it does not by itself backpropagate `dr/dx` through discrete generated outputs. The causal gradient explanation is thus **UNVERIFIED** without training implementation evidence.

**Limits and potential reward hacking.** [Vero §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04917v3) directly documents self-evaluative text and fabricated measurements in preliminary judged caption runs, then reports judge-prompt penalties. Their statement that guidelines suffice is an **author interpretation** for their setting, not a medical safety guarantee. MRI numeric rewards can be hacked by learning workbook priors, metadata leakage, broad tolerances, or outputting plausible but image-unsupported changes; categorical rewards can reward dataset prevalence; 2D boxes can ignore slice/world coordinates. Guard with image-swap, visit-swap, no-image, metadata-only, wrong-subject, altered-spacing, and sub-repeatability perturbations plus evaluator independence. [MedGRPO §4/Appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06581v4) concerns procedural video, not native 3D/longitudinal MRI; training-code parity, patient/video split, multi-seed uncertainty and judge independence are **UNVERIFIED** here.

## Local project transfer and novelty boundary

The [project thesis](../RESEARCH_THESIS.md) asks whether task-balanced *online verifiable RL* improves patient-separated brain MRI task transfer while retaining grounding/explanation quality. The Dallas pilot has [2 subjects, 4 sessions, 8 verified volumes](../../data/SUBJECT_STUDY_LINEAGE.md), including T1 and FLAIR at two waves, but no independent masks, longitudinal repeatability threshold, model outputs or training. One FLAIR pair changes through-plane spacing 6 to 2.5 mm. These facts establish access and render feasibility, **not** MRI reward validity.

Actual local code is scaffolding: [`core.py`](../../src/mri_rl/core.py) defines exact categorical/direction, thresholded numeric and *frame-aware 2D IoU* rewards, plus `balanced_sample` with replacement. It is neither Vero's ten-route implementation nor a 3D mask/registered-change verifier. `longitudinal_direction` accepts a target string without verifying measurement provenance or repeatability. [`experiments.py`](../../src/mri_rl/experiments.py) checks equal `(steps, group_size, max_tokens)` only; it does not enforce equal actual visual tokens, example/subject exposure, wall-clock/FLOPs, updates after zero-variance dropping, or checkpoint identity. [`feasibility_arms.json`](../../configs/feasibility_arms.json) is marked `NOT_RUN`. `evaluate` omits unanswered rows from score denominators and reports coverage separately; a future prespecified primary outcome should penalize or otherwise explicitly handle abstentions/missing outputs. No claimed MRI RL result follows from this code.

**Novelty verdict:** `Related Prior Art` for Vero-style balanced, heterogeneous visual RL (Vero), medical multitask GRPO with nonlinear reward calibration (MedGRPO), and improvement-aware worst-task weighting plus ratio-preserving effective-gradient sampling (MT-GRPO). The inspected sources do not establish direct *longitudinal 3D brain MRI* use of the exact combined objective, but this bounded search is insufficient for a global priority claim. Reusing either sampler, optimizer, generic IoU, sigmoid, or dynamic zero-advantage-aware weighting is not algorithmic novelty. A credible contribution needs a verified MRI-specific mechanism and a controlled benefit beyond strong existing calibration, MT-GRPO and same-data SFT.

## Layer 3 — ranked research cards

### Card 1 — verifier validity before optimization (highest priority)

**Unresolved gap / scientific question:** Can a machine-verifiable reward detect *image-supported* 3D or paired-visit change rather than metadata or acquisition artifacts? **Hypothesis:** a provenance-bound, physical-coordinate, repeatability-aware verifier rejects subject/visit swaps and changes below test–retest variability while rewarding independently annotated visible changes. **Potential method:** structured target with subject/session lineage, registered 3D region/mask, voxel-world affine, acquisition/QC state and predeclared uncertainty band. **Minimal experiment:** enlarge Dallas to a subject-disjoint set; obtain independent labels or validated quantitative references; score genuine cases and negative controls (no-image, wrong subject, swapped visits, slice-spacing perturbation, sham change) before training. **Baselines:** existing string/direction reward, 2D IoU, metadata-only rules, independent image-based classical measurement. **Evaluation:** false-positive rate on controls, sensitivity on real changes, calibration around repeatability limit, adjudicator agreement; patient is the unit. **GO:** low prespecified false-positive bound and clear benefit over metadata/2D controls on independent references. **KILL:** reward can be earned from nonimage clues or cannot distinguish real changes from acquisition variation. **Next action:** define one independently verifiable anatomical endpoint and its repeatability protocol. This card is scientifically essential but higher data/annotation cost than a replay check.

### Card 2 — distinguish uniform sampling from reward calibration

**Gap / question:** Is a uniform MRI task mix helpful after MedGRPO-style nonlinear calibration and MT-GRPO-style group-quality filtering? **Hypothesis:** balanced sampling improves held-out task-macro performance and reduces off-target regression beyond proportional sampling *at the same reward calibration and exposure*. **Potential method:** patient-stratified category sampling plus provenance-checked rewards; no new optimizer initially. **Minimal experiment:** first replay frozen groups under raw/positive-affine/logistic/rank rewards to inspect advantage differences, group variance and zero-variance rate; then paired online arms from one fixed checkpoint and subject pool: proportional/raw, uniform/raw, proportional/logistic, uniform/logistic, each with/without a prespecified zero-variance rule. Include uniform ratio-preserving post-filter sampling and MT-GRPO improvement-aware sampling when the valid cohort makes multi-task RL possible. **Baselines:** same-data SFT, single-task RL, calibrated proportional RL, uniform plus ratio-preserving sampler, full MT-GRPO. **Evaluation:** patient-held-out macro and per-task scores, worst-task delta, desired versus realized post-filter task share, failed/zero-variance rollout fraction, actual visual tokens, optimizer updates, wall-clock and seed variation. **GO:** uniform gains remain across seeds under logistic/rank and equal effective gradient/visual-token budgets with no clinically important task loss. **KILL:** benefit disappears after calibration/filtering or MT-GRPO, is driven by extra unique subjects or rollouts, or worsens independent endpoints. **Next action:** collect real model rollout groups only after Card 1 reward gate. Innovation risk is high because Vero, MedGRPO and MT-GRPO supply the generic ingredients.

### Card 3 — sequence-level versus token-level optimization on long MRI answers

**Gap / question:** Does GSPO's sequence-ratio clipping improve stability for long multi-slice MRI reasoning after the same calibrated reward? **Hypothesis:** with dense MRI VLMs and fixed rewards, GSPO-token maintains more usable updates and less off-target degradation than GRPO at matched compute. **Potential method:** same verifier, task samples, checkpoint, rollouts, evaluator and model, changing only ratio/clipping implementation with suitably tuned clip ranges. **Minimal experiment:** small patient-separated, equal-budget two-arm pilot after Card 1 and Card 2; log sequence/token clip fractions, length, entropy, zero-variance groups, effective gradients and endpoints. **Baselines:** token GRPO, GSPO-token, same-data SFT. **GO:** reproducible patient-level improvement and stability beyond tuned GRPO, not explained by unequal clipping or answer length. **KILL:** no difference or benefit only under untuned GRPO, or no independent MRI endpoint gain. **Next action:** choose the checkpoint and publish a matched hyperparameter grid. Innovation risk is very high because GSPO itself is direct optimizer prior art; the finding may be an empirical domain result rather than a new algorithm.

**Overall gate:** GO for larger data and Card 1; conditionally GO for Cards 2–3 after labels and GPU access. Current `MODEL_INFERENCE`, `RL_TRAINING`, and method-transfer outcomes remain `NOT_RUN`. The Dallas two-subject access pilot cannot estimate any of the proposed effects.
