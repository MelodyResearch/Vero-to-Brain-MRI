# Vero — primary-source methodological audit

## Verified metadata and access

*Vero: An Open RL Recipe for General Visual Reasoning*, Gabriel Sarch, Linrong Cai, Qunzhong Wang, Haoyang Wu, Danqi Chen, Zhuang Liu. `arXiv:2604.04917v3`, revised 18 June 2026 (v1 submitted 6 April); [versioned full HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04917v3), [43-page PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.04917), [official repository](https://github.com/zlab-princeton/vero), [data format and routing documentation](https://github.com/zlab-princeton/vero/blob/main/docs/DATA.md), [training documentation](https://github.com/zlab-princeton/vero/blob/main/docs/TRAINING.md). The repo announces a later 1.6M/2.5M data release, while this audit focuses on the v3 paper's **Vero-600K** experiments. Full Methods, Results, Appendix, PDF tables and repository docs were read; repository code execution is **NOT_VERIFIED**. PDF pages below are one-based, e.g., web P10 = PDF p. 11.

## Layer 1 — Research process

The documented problem is broad visual reasoning where earlier open RL recipes overconcentrate on math/STEM or narrow perceptual tasks and cannot reward heterogeneous answer types (§§1–2). Vero's gap is an **open, reproducible data/reward/training recipe** that learns across six visual task categories while preserving open-ended chat. It is not a new GSPO optimizer or a medical/3D MRI model.

The authors selected 59 of 250+ candidate datasets, then filtered small/low-resolution/binary sets, manually inspected about 50 examples per candidate, used Qwen3-VL-235B to reject ambiguous or image-irrelevant questions, and canonicalized answers for verifier stability (§3.2, Fig. 3, PDF pp. 4–6). Filtering helps some domains and slightly harms others (Table 1, PDF p. 6), so the curation contribution is the pipeline and mix, not uniformly beneficial filtering. Vero-600K spans Chart/OCR, STEM, Spatial/Action, Knowledge/Recognition, Grounding/Counting/Search, and Captioning/Instruction Following. Uniform **category sampling** is chosen because Table 2, PDF p. 6 gives +5.8 overall gain versus +5.2 difficulty-, +5.2 image-area-, and +4.8 reasoning-length weighting on a Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct starting model. Uniformity is an empirical choice for those data/task weights, not a theorem or a guarantee for MRI.

The RL algorithm inherits GRPO's group-relative advantages and GSPO's sequence-ratio clipped objective (Eq. 2, §3.3, PDF p. 7), plus asymmetric clip-higher, no KL penalty and soft overlong penalty from cited prior work. The total reward is 0.8 accuracy + 0.2 format + overlong term (Eq. 3–4, PDF p. 7). The *new recipe integration* is routing `reward_type` to ten verifiers: exact, MCQ, numeric, list, ordering, web action, frame-independent 2D grounding, clicking, instruction following, and LLM judge (§3.3; repo `docs/DATA.md`). The answer format adds nonempty think/answer tags and boxed answers for symbolic tasks. Some verifiers are adapted from Visual Jigsaw, ViGoRL, Perception-R1, and other prior code, and the judge from OLMo3; Vero does not originate them. The repo explicitly stores `reward_type`, `data_source`, `domain`, and canonical `answer` per training sample; routing is by metadata, not inferred task identity.

Alternatives considered in the paper include SFT, a single `math_verify` reward, GRPO/DAPO, category specialists, and four weighting rules. A further credible alternative that was **not isolated** is task-aware reward normalization/filtering without uniform sampling, or a single robust verifier with equivalent answer coverage. In MRI, numeric tolerance would need units, error bounds, and provenance; 2D box IoU cannot verify 3D segmentation or longitudinal clinical change.

Figure 6's comparison to ViRL-39K, OpenMMReasoner-74K and LLaVA-OV-1.5-RL-70K uses a shared Qwen2.5-VL-7B initialization and RL settings for 600 steps over three seeds (§4.1, PDF p. 11), a stronger controlled comparison than heterogeneous model leaderboards. Dataset sizes and exposure still differ: Vero-600K can provide more unique samples than 39K–74K sources over the same steps. The 600K mixture and thirty-benchmark suite are broad **natural-image** VLM training/evaluation, not a volumetric MRI experiment. The paper's use of an LLM judge for captioning is vulnerable to reward hacking; §6 reports self-evaluative text and fabricated measurement details, then adds judge rules. This is a caution, not proof such hacking is fully eliminated on unseen medical content. No patient-level, scan-level, or longitudinal validity is established by Vero.

## Layer 3 — Next experiment

**Question:** In real brain MRI, does balanced task mixing prevent off-target loss because of task diversity, or only because of reward scale and open-ended data exposure? **Hypothesis:** with the same subject-disjoint MRI examples, initialization, update count, rollout count, image tokens, and calibrated verifier scale, a balanced mix reduces spatial/quantitative/caption regression versus single-task RL while improving its target metrics. The cheapest decisive first step is **no training**: audit each proposed reward against known mistakes, invalid coordinates, text-only predictions, and changed scan order; measure reward variance per task. If it fails, RL is not justified.

For training, compare (1) base, (2) same-data SFT, (3) single-task RL, (4) naturally proportioned multi-task RL, (5) uniform categories, (6) uniform with reward-scale normalization, and (7) category mix with open-ended task removed. Use actual 3D MRI/paired visits and independently validated masks or quantitative measurements, not self-derived reward as sole evaluation truth. Predefine patient as inference unit, prespecified primary outcome per task, and paired patient bootstrap. Crucial ablations: category allocation, route choice, box-vs-voxel verifier, reward normalization, missing/invalid answer handling, caption judge. GO if balanced RL improves prespecified held-out patient outcomes beyond matched controls without capability loss; REFORMULATE if benefit disappears after normalization or comes only from extra training data; KILL the proposed multi-task RL method if strong SFT and simple normalization match it or reward hacking dominates.

## Independent novelty and MRI transfer

**Vero novelty location:** curated training data, category mixture and **integrated** reward-routing recipe with broad controlled evaluation. **Inherited:** GSPO/GRPO, clip-higher, overlong penalty, 2D Hungarian box reward, generic numeric/string/list rewards, LLM judge, base VLMs. **Necessary technical difference:** jointly applying heterogeneous, metadata-routed rewards to a balanced multi-domain RL corpus and preserving visual chat by explicitly training open-ended tasks. **Remaining MRI gap:** true 3D spatial correspondence, cross-sequence interpretation, calibrated repeated-scan change, subject lineage, and independent verifiers. Unchanged transfer is plausible only for abstract scaffolding—sample metadata, routing interface, optimizer, and controlled ablation logic. New MRI-specific verifiers must respect voxel-to-world coordinates, spacing, masks, registration, uncertainty, and acquisition changes. Merely replacing Vero's visual data with MRI and retaining task categories is an **application/replication risk**. A genuine contribution would show a verified capability on longitudinal or volumetric MRI that Vero's 2D rewards and strong same-data SFT cannot deliver, with negative-transfer mitigation beyond ordinary category/reward normalization.

## Missing evidence and uncertainties

No clinical MRI result or 3D Dice/voxel reward. Exact equivalence of judge prompts, unique-example exposure, and compute across *all* public baselines is **NOT_VERIFIED**. The official repo is live and its later release differs from the v3 600K method; a replication must pin commit, dataset revision, checkpoint, CUDA stack, and judge model before execution. None of the cited Vero scores are our reproduced outcomes.
