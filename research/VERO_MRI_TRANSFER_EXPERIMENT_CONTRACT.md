# Prespecified Vero-style cross-task MRI transfer experiment contract

**Status (2026-10-15): DESIGN ONLY — training and transfer matrix `NOT_RUN`.** Independent Statistical and Experiment Agent review. I read the installed global Norman paper-reading skill and evidence/novelty references, the local thesis, hypothesis and source-access audits, and the Vero/GSPO/MedGRPO/MT-GRPO method review. This contract defines how to test the project's **primary question**: whether task-balanced online verifiable RL improves transfer among distinct brain-MRI task categories. Longitudinal biological change is an optional category only after its label and verifier pass their own validity gate; it is **not** a prerequisite for testing transfer among other eligible MRI tasks. This is a proposed design, not an observed result.

## 1. Target estimands, units and task admission

Let `T` contain at least two scientifically distinct, image-linked and program-verifiable brain-MRI categories. Candidate examples are anatomical category recognition, spatial localization on independently checked 2D slices or 3D masks, and numerical/counting tasks with defensible references. A longitudinal direction category may enter only after independent repeatability-qualified targets exist. Two labels on the same image that share the same answer information do not constitute two independent tasks. For every category, lock a **task card** before training: input images and metadata allowed, target provenance, verifier code/version, answerability from allowed evidence, reference uncertainty, forbidden shortcuts, acquisition mix, patient linkage, train/validation/test counts, missingness, and licenses. Text-only labels, source reports in the prompt, bounding boxes selected from hidden test masks, and dataset prevalence shortcuts fail admission. A category can be studied as an engineering diagnostic without entering the scientific transfer matrix.

The evaluation unit is a **patient within a task and source cohort**. Multiple visits, scans, slices, regions, questions and model rollouts from the same patient form a cluster. For task `t`, model `m`, seed `s`, calculate a prespecified patient score `Y_{p,t,m,s}` by aggregating all eligible items for that patient (with item type weights fixed on training data). Estimate a task effect as the mean *paired* patient difference over the same held-out patients. Report the number of patients as well as scans/items. A task with unknown patient IDs may be shown descriptively but cannot support a patient-generalization claim. Do not let a large QA category dominate merely by having more questions per patient.

Separate sources may supply different task categories. Their patient IDs are not automatically comparable or globally unique. Namespace identifiers by source, audit common upstream datasets, visit dates and any privacy-safe linkage available, and mark unresolved cross-source duplication `UNVERIFIED`. Keep each source's patients disjoint across its own train/validation/test, and avoid pooling potentially overlapping sources across those partitions until linkage can be checked. If cross-source identity cannot be established, use source-isolated training and evaluation, state that cross-cohort independence is unresolved, and do not claim patient-independent external generalization. A mixed-task result across separate cohorts estimates transfer among **task-plus-source domains**: task effects and scanner/cohort effects are confounded. To identify task transfer more cleanly, add at least one cohort with two eligible task labels on the same held-out patients, or replicate a category across sources and test leave-source-out behavior. Such corroboration is a target for stronger claims, not a reason to block an initial bounded matrix.

## 2. Matrix and primary outcomes

For `K=|T|` admitted categories, train `K` single-category RL arms and score **every** checkpoint on **every** held-out category. Publish the complete `K × K` matrix: row = training category, column = evaluation category. Add rows for starting base, same-data SFT, proportional mixture, uniform mixture and calibrated/active-group controls. Use identical frozen test cases and scorer for all rows. Off-diagonal changes relative to the common starting checkpoint quantify transfer/interference; diagonal changes alone are specialization. If a task's input interface is unsupported by one arm, record `NOT_EVALUABLE` and exclude that task from the prespecified primary matrix, rather than replacing it after observing results.

Prespecify two co-primary effects of **uniform versus proportional** training under the same reward calibration and active-group policy:

1. **Equal-category macro effect:** average of patient-mean task effects, with one weight per admitted category. Publish each component; this is an intentional research objective, not an estimate of natural clinical prevalence.
2. **Worst-category safety effect:** the smallest task-specific patient-mean difference (`uniform − proportional`) and its simultaneous uncertainty bound. This guards against a macro win that harms spatial grounding, abstention or hallucination-sensitive categories. Safety endpoints such as unsupported anatomical assertions or wrong-laterality acceptance must be prespecified as separate gates rather than hidden inside accuracy.

Secondary outcomes: all off-diagonal cell differences; task-specific accuracy/IoU/Dice/numeric error as applicable; abstention and coverage; false supported-claim acceptance; calibration; acquisition strata; per-patient and per-task loss/regression tails; realized post-filter task mixture; informative-group rate; optimizer updates; unique-patient exposure; generated and visual-token count; wall time. Never collapse incompatible task metrics directly. Standardize or map each task score using a training/validation-only rule fixed before test, and retain original units. If a task metric cannot be put on an interpretable common scale, report the vector and worst-task criterion instead of an arbitrary macro number.

## 3. Arms and causal contrasts

All **newly trained** arms start from the same pinned base model and processor revision. A frozen `Qwen/Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct` versus frozen `zlab-princeton/Vero-Qwen3I-8B` comparison, if executable, is an *external pretrained transfer* diagnostic; Vero has different prior data/training and is **not** a causal comparator for the newly trained arm contrasts. Pin model revisions, renderer, prompt/output schema, image and visual-token budget, decoding, answer parser and test scorer. Do not mix different model families or inspect sealed test data during arm selection.

| Arm | Role | Required comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Base frozen | No local training reference | All tasks, no-image and image controls. |
| Same-data domain SFT | Strong non-RL alternative | Same eligible training patients/data exposure and a documented compute budget. |
| RL task `t` only, for each `t ∈ T` | `K` specialist rows | Full `K × K` evaluation; same start and total RL budget. |
| Proportional mixture + raw task reward | Natural-size mix baseline | The exact training category proportions are recorded before training. |
| Uniform mixture + raw task reward | Vero-style category balance | Primary sampling contrast against proportional/raw. |
| Proportional mixture + MedGRPO-style nonlinear calibration | Reward-calibration control | Training-set percentile/IQR parameters only; same rollout groups where replay is possible. |
| Uniform mixture + identical calibration | Factorial interaction | Separates sampling effect from reward transform. |
| Uniform + ratio-preserving active-group sampling | Effective-gradient baseline | Controls for zero-variance filtering changing realized task weights. |
| Full MT-GRPO-style dynamic task weighting, if feasible | Strong prior-art control | Distinguishes fixed uniform from improvement-aware weighting. |

The minimum first training stage is **base, SFT, each specialist, and 2×2 proportional/uniform × raw/calibrated** after task admission. A staged diagnostic can first replay saved rollout groups without policy updates; its outputs are optimizer-mechanism evidence, **not** transfer performance. A later optimizer comparison (GRPO versus GSPO) varies only optimizer ratio/clipping implementation after category and reward effects are understood; Vero's GSPO is inherited prior art. Both within-task reward route and answer format weights must be identical across the 2×2 arms. Positive affine reward transforms are a replay control because ideal group-standardized advantages are invariant to them (up to epsilon/implementation effects); they do not constitute a separate claimed method. For all-equal groups, a monotone calibration cannot create informative advantages. Log ties and the policy for skipping/resampling them.

## 4. Matched-budget and split contract

Define **two budget estimands**, because holding actual generated tokens and effective optimizer updates simultaneously fixed may require different resampling:

- **Compute-matched policy effect (primary):** fixed maximum generated text+visual tokens or measured accelerator time/FLOPs, fixed sequence limits, fixed starting weights and unique training-patient pool. Report effective updates and realized task proportions as outcomes. This tests whether a method gives more benefit per practical budget.
- **Update-matched mechanism sensitivity:** equal accepted/informative rollout groups and optimizer updates, with extra generations/time paid by methods that filter ties. Report the extra cost. This isolates optimization from sample-efficiency cost.

Nominal `steps × group_size × max_tokens` is insufficient: images produce variable visual tokens, task prompts vary, zero-variance groups can be discarded, and balanced sampling with replacement can repeatedly expose the same patient. Log per arm and task: attempted groups, accepted groups, zero-variance groups, actual completions, visual/text tokens, wall time, optimizer steps, examples and **unique patients**, repeat exposure distribution, checkpoint and reward version. An arm with extra training subjects or hidden label fields fails the matched contrast. Report both intent-to-train and post-filter realized category weights. If task cohorts differ in size, cap or explicitly account for unique-patient exposure so an apparent uniform benefit is not simply oversampling a tiny cohort.

Allocate patients to train/calibration/test *before* preprocessing decisions and prompt/reward tuning. Keep all same-patient visits/modalities/regions within one partition and all derived images/QA linked to it. Use a separate validation set for parser, reward thresholds, stop time and hyperparameter selection; lock one final checkpoint/selection rule before the sealed test. BrainTRACE is test-only if authorized and must never tune task admission, prompts, reward or checkpoint. SGMRI/fastMRI images require approved source access, and its patient identity must be verified rather than assuming volume ID is patient ID. NeuroQA QA text alone is insufficient for image-linked training. Dallas's currently verified 12 T1-pair subjects are a real-data engineering set, not a statistically adequate train/validation/test matrix.

Use training seeds paired across arms (same seed list and, where feasible, same initial sampling stream). Predeclare enough seeds to estimate training instability; do not treat stochastic completions or repeated slices as independent replicates. Inference decoding randomness is nested within patient and checkpoint. Freeze test item eligibility and all task directions so a failed or unevaluable task cannot be dropped after seeing a matrix.

## 5. Uncertainty, power and multiplicity

Before training, choose a scientifically meaningful macro gain `δ_macro`, acceptable worst-task loss `η_worst`, and safety failure thresholds from task-specific measurement/expert requirements. Do not select these margins from held-out outcomes. A pilot on **training/validation patients only** estimates cluster-level variance, within-patient item correlation, task prevalence, no-image baseline and expected abstention. Use those estimates to simulate patient-level power for paired model differences under anticipated seed variability and dropout; state feasible sample size by task and source. No fixed `n` can be honestly derived from the current 12 age-targeted Dallas cases or from published question counts. If enough patients cannot be obtained, report the experiment as feasibility/descriptive and do not convert many QA items into nominal power.

For each arm/task/seed, first average within patient. Compute paired patient-level contrasts on the same held-out patients. Use a patient-cluster bootstrap or an appropriate hierarchical model for per-task intervals, then account separately for training-seed variation; report both patient uncertainty and between-seed spread. If cohorts are disjoint across tasks, resample patients **within each cohort/task** and combine the resulting task effects under the fixed equal-category weighting. Do not bootstrap a pooled bag of questions. For the worst-category minimum and multiple off-diagonal tests, use simultaneous/bootstrap max-type bounds or a prespecified multiplicity procedure; raw per-cell 95% intervals are exploratory. Report exact denominators, coverage, missing predictions and invalid outputs, and a primary analysis that penalizes missing/abstention according to a preregistered rule. Conditional accuracy among answered cases is secondary.

Gate the primary claims hierarchically: first verify admitted task/reward validity and no source leakage; then test macro improvement; then require the worst-task lower bound above `−η_worst` and each safety endpoint within its allowed regression. Separately test whether the uniform effect persists under MedGRPO calibration and ratio-preserving/MT-GRPO controls. A statistically positive macro average with a grounding decline does not pass. If seed-to-seed variation dominates the estimated benefit or the lower bound fails the prespecified meaningful gain, classify the method effect `INCONCLUSIVE` or `KILL` according to the declared decision margin, not as an unqualified success.

## 6. Interpretation and decision rules

**GO for first transfer training** when at least two genuinely distinct tasks have authorized linked MRI images, patient-grouped train/validation/test, answerable targets, reliable independent held-out scoring, a functional verifier challenge, and compatible compute. This gate does not require longitudinal biological-change labels if the admitted tasks are anatomical or spatial. **GO for a Vero-style transfer finding** only if the full matrix shows a reproducible, patient-level cross-task benefit versus specialists and proportional mixing at matched compute, with the macro margin met, worst-task safety retained, shortcut controls defeated and no calibration/active-group explanation. **GO for AI method innovation** additionally requires a distinct MRI-specific operation or learning mechanism that beats the closest Vero/MedGRPO/MT-GRPO and simple deterministic alternatives; uniform mixing itself is prior art.

**REFORMULATE** if task-plus-source effects cannot be disentangled, a task is solved equally by metadata/no-image, or reward calibration/active-group handling explains the uniform advantage. Report a valuable benchmark or data-engineering result at its proper level. **KILL a particular reward/task** if its target is not image-answerable or it accepts unsupported outputs. **KILL the distinct balance claim** if calibrated proportional or active-group baselines match it within the prespecified meaningful margin, after equal actual budget and patient/seed uncertainty. Negative transfer on one task is a primary result, not a reason to omit that cell. A failed longitudinal-change category does not by itself kill the broader cross-task MRI experiment.

For each completed matrix cell, record: **observed result → supported conclusion → competing explanation → scientific consequence → next experiment**. Cross-task gains on separate sources support an MRI *task-plus-domain* transfer finding. A change in independently adjudicated anatomical findings on held-out patients supports a narrower MRI scientific claim. Neither automatically establishes clinical utility or a novel optimizer.
